Two constraints that will not move

Both measured. Worth knowing before spending effort against them.

  • The LiDAR cannot find roof creases. The survey is 1.7 returns/m² — about 0.77 m spacing — and the 1 m DSM is already at its native resolution. A hip crease falls between samples. So imagery must find the lines and LiDAR fits the angles, which is the split you described.
  • The detector emits stubs, not folds. Where you draw a 5 m fold it finds 1.5 m at low confidence; 98.5% of its line endpoints dangle, against 56.4% of yours. That single fact explains both roofs you flagged — missing folds on one, invented lines on the other.

Traps worth not rediscovering

Each of these cost real time or a wrong conclusion this week.

  • --limit 30 is not a sample. Building ids are sorted, so it is every 47xxxxx building. A change once won on 30 roofs and lost on all 85. Sample randomly or run the whole set.
  • Facet agreement cannot see a missing building. It scores shape against your drawings and has no idea whether a roof ships any panels at all. Three regressions passed it and were caught only against the live site.
  • Never overwrite a running shell script. Bash reads by byte offset; the running driver resumed at a shifted position and re-entered its own loop. Stage to a new filename.
  • A/B harnesses that silently measure nothing. Three times a test toggled one input while the code read another. Assert the toggle changed the output before trusting a result.
  • Selecting on the signal biases the check. A “75-roof regression” was 3 once the flagged roofs stopped being the sample.
More labelling still pays: boundary accuracy rises about 1.1 F1 per 100 roofs and is still climbing — roughly 106 more roofs to reach 0.30.
