The benchmark is 152 roofs and only 11 are drawn, so it can see a big regression but not a small one. Drawing 40–50 roofs in that one cluster would turn it into dense ground truth where a 2% change is visible immediately.

Draw sawtooth and repeated-fold roofs

Where the detector fails hardest — it finds a building's long axes and misses every cross-fold. Draw each fold all the way across; a complete line teaches far more than a short one.

Constraints that will not move

  • The LiDAR cannot find roof creases. The survey is 1.7 returns/m² — about 0.77 m spacing — and the 1 m DSM is already at native resolution, so a hip crease falls between samples. Imagery must find the lines and LiDAR fit the angles.
  • The detector emits stubs, not folds. Where you draw a 5 m fold it finds 1.5 m; 98.5% of its line endpoints dangle against 56.4% of yours. More labelled roofs measurably help: boundary F1 has gone 0.122 → 0.185 as labels grew 18 → 74, about +1.1 per 100 roofs, still climbing.
