Zoom: scroll where the cursor is, or the + − buttons top-right.
Pan: press ✋ Pan and drag, or drag with the right button, or hold Space and drag, or use the arrow keys (hold Shift for bigger steps).

Three lines — click, then click

How to draw lines
Each line is its own segment — two clicks and it’s done. Esc abandons a half-drawn one. Hold Shift to snap to 15°.
Endpoints snap — a white circle means an exact corner, a white diamond means anywhere along an existing line or the outline. Use the diamond for a T-junction, and to land a line exactly on the roof edge.
When a shaded face is not really roof — a wall, a canopy — press 8 and click it.
No need to trace roof edges — the outline has them, and anything the outline overshoots onto at ground level is deleted automatically. Only draw an edge where the roof stops short and what is beyond is still high (an upper deck, a carport).
About this tool

Faces that take no panels

What counts as no-panel
Your lines shade the roof into faces as you draw. Click any face that could never take a panel — a wall caught inside the outline, a canopy, glass. Click again to unmark.
This is the one that catches the worst failure we have: panels placed down a wall. If a shaded face is not really roof, this is how you say so.

Obstructions — drag out a shape

See what the build currently makes of this roof
Press B to show the facets the pipeline produced for this roof, dashed in blue underneath your lines. It is a reference, not a tracing guide — where it disagrees with the roof, draw what you see, not what it drew. A labeller who traces the pipeline's mistakes teaches the model its own errors.
If nothing appears, this roof has not been built yet.
How to draw obstructions
Polygon and Line / run: click each point, then Enter (a polygon also closes if you click its first point again). Triangle: three clicks. Circle: drag one out.
A line / run records a centreline with a width — [ and ] make it thinner or wider. Esc abandons a part-drawn shape, ⌘Z removes the last point.
Drag a shape’s middle to move it, a corner to resize, Delete removes the selected one.

Fix a mistake

How selecting works
Click any line or shape you have already drawn to select it, then press Delete (or the button below). Undo only ever removes the most recent thing; this reaches the one you actually want.

Something wrong with this roof?

Which one to use
Nothing is there — bare ground, a demolished or cleared site. The survey is from 2021 and the outlines have not caught up. These get excluded from the build entirely.
Not a building — something real but not a roof: a slab, a container, a pool, a tank, tree canopy the outline has mistaken for a structure.
Outline is wrong — a building IS there, but this outline is offset, or merges two buildings, or splits one. The roof is fine; the footprint is not. If it is only slightly off, do not bother flagging it — just mark the roof where it really is. That is the more useful record, and the offset is measurable without you.
Cannot read the image — too blurred, too dark, too deep in shadow to see the folds. Flag it and move on. A guessed line is worse than no line, because it becomes truth that the model is scored against.

A roof being complicated is NOT a reason to skip it. Cut-up roofs are the ones the model gets wrong, so they are the most valuable thing you can mark. If you can see the shape, mark it — whether much solar fits is for the model to work out, and it can only do that from the geometry.

Any of these marks the roof done, so it stops coming back. Do not draw lines on it — there is nothing to learn from geometry that is not a roof.

Is this roof finished?

Why this matters
Only tick this if you drew every fold and break you can see, not just the main ones. It is what makes "the model drew a line that is not real" measurable — without it, a line you simply did not get to looks identical to one the model invented. Leaving it unticked is fine and costs nothing.

Actions

Where your marks go
Saved in this browser as you go, so closing the tab is safe. Mark as few or as many as you like — Download only includes roofs you actually touched.

Three kinds of line

Seen from above, which is how you’ll be looking at them.
Ridge line
Any fold where the roof falls away on both sides. Flat along the top (a ridge) or running downhill to a corner (a hip) — both go here. They are the same thing to the model, so do not spend time deciding which. A roof shaped angle–flat–angle has several of these; mark them all.
Valley line
Any other fold — the two faces meet at an angle, but the roof does not turn over the top. Two common shapes:
• an internal corner, where two roof sections join, most often on an L or T shaped house;
• a change of pitch, where a steep face runs into a shallow one — say 60° into 20° — and the roof keeps falling the same way, just less steeply.

Ignore where water goes. Plenty of valley lines are nowhere near a gutter and nothing would ever collect in them; the pitch-change case above is the obvious one. What the model needs is that the surface folds here, not that it drains here.
Cliff line
Not a fold at all — the surface stops and the height jumps. Three cases, all the same thing:
• a stepped or split-level roof;
• the sides of something raised — a dormer, clerestory, plant box, lift overrun;
• where the roof itself ends short of the yellow outline — see below.

How to draw it: put the line along the top edge of the higher surface, following the lip where it drops away. For a dormer, that means drawing round its sides — one line per side.
The quick test, in order:
1. Does the surface stop and the height jump? Cliff line.
2. Otherwise it is a fold. Does the roof turn over here — falling away on both sides, whether along the top or running down to a corner? Ridge line.
3. It folds but does not turn over. Valley line.

There are only three because that is all the code actually uses: does the surface break (stops and drops), or fold — and if it folds, does it turn over or not? Ridge versus hip, or which folds a builder would call valleys, is vocabulary the pipeline never consults, so it is not worth your time.

Roof edges: mostly leave them alone. Do not trace edges that sit on the yellow outline — those are already known. And do not trace an edge just because the outline overshoots onto a driveway, patio or deck at ground level: every panel already has to stand 2 m above the local ground, measured against LiDAR ground returns, so those are deleted without your help.

There is one case worth your time: the roof stops short and what is beyond is still high — an upper-storey deck or balcony, a carport, a shade sail. Those clear 2 m and are flat, so the LiDAR reads them as roof and nothing downstream can tell the difference. Draw the real roof edge as a Cliff line, then switch to No panels here and click the strip outside it.
If you are unsure whether the thing beyond is high or on the ground, skip it — the height test will handle it if it is low, and a wrong label is worse than a missing one.

If you cannot tell whether a line is a fold or a break at all, leave it out rather than guess — a wrong label is worse than a missing one.
